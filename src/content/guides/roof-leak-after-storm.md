**Short answer:** When your Sioux Falls roof leaks after a storm, start by protecting what is inside. Catch the water, move your things, and take photos. Stay off the roof. Then look for clues from the ground and from the attic, and call a roofer with what you found.

*Published November 9, 2026*

A leak can feel like a big deal, and sometimes it is. Often, though, it comes from one small spot. Here is how to keep things under control and get good information.

## What to do first

1. **Make the area safe.** If a ceiling is bulging or sagging, keep everyone out from under it. If water is near lights or outlets, stay clear of that area.
2. **Catch the water.** Put down a bucket and towels, and move furniture and anything you care about.
3. **Take photos.** Get the stain, the drip, and anything damaged. Write down the date and when the storm came through.
4. **Stay off the roof.** Wet and storm damaged roofs are slippery and may be weaker than they look.

## What you can check from the ground

- Look for missing, lifted, or folded shingles.
- Check gutters, vents, and your air conditioner for dents.
- Look around your downspouts and yard for shingle pieces or heavy grit.
- Walk around the house and check the siding and window trim on the side that faced the storm.

## What you can check in the attic

Only go up if you have a safe way to get there and solid places to step. Bring a flashlight. Look for wet insulation, dark stains on the boards, and light coming through. Note where they are compared with the stain on your ceiling. Water often runs along a rafter, so the leak may be higher up than the stain.

## What the clues usually mean

| What you notice | Common cause |
|---|---|
| Stain near a chimney or wall | Worn or loose flashing |
| Drip near a bathroom or kitchen | Cracked rubber boot around a vent pipe |
| Leak only in strong wind | Rain pushed under shingles or at the roof edge |
| Water along an outside wall in winter | Possible ice dam |
| Shingles in the yard | Wind damage that needs a closer look |

## When to call

Call when water is getting in, when you see damage from the ground, or when you just are not sure. Tell us your town, where you see the water, and when it started. If you have photos, have them handy.
